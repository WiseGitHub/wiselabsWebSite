TIP • 01
Tutorial •Sample Production

Clean Sample Envelopes:
Transient In, Tail Out

Clicks happen when amplitude changes too abruptly. The goal is simple: enter and leave the sound smoothly, without needlessly weakening its transient or its tail.

  1. Attack • use the shortest fade that removes the click. Typical 0.1-2 ms for percussion; longer for low-end or badly-cut material. Judge transient loss against the remaining click.
  2. Decay • shapes the transition from the peak down to the sustain level. Steep = tighter, more percussive; slower or curved = softer, more natural. Still applies even when sustain is zero.
  3. Sustain • the level held while the note stays active. A level, not a duration. High for held sounds, low for plucks, zero for one-shot-style envelopes.
  4. Release • returns smoothly to silence when the note is let go. Short = controlled; longer = more spacious. Depends on the playback mode, and very short releases can click.
  5. Check the boundaries • make sure the gain envelope reaches zero amplitude at both ends. For loops, inspect both loop points and crossfade if needed. Also watch for DC offset and abrupt processing changes.
About

WiseLabs is a French music producer and sound designer.

It started in 1998 on Music, Jester Interactive's studio-in-a-game for PlayStation, with tracks bounced to cassette through the family hi-fi and a tangle of RCA cables, then moved to the PC and DAWs by 2000, settling into a long-running preference for FL Studio, Image-Line's DAW, started in 1997 in Belgium.

Plenty of others came through along the way: Hammerhead, ModPlug Tracker (now OpenMPT), Guitar Pro, Live, Reason, Renoise, Bitwig, and a long tail of smaller music-production tools, picked up for whatever sound design they made possible.

A multi-instrumentalist by ear: drums and anything percussive, keys, synths and pianos, guitars, basses and other strings, plus pads and controllers. That hands-on range feeds the sound design, building instruments and effects entirely from native devices and recordings from various devices or situations rather than reaching for third-party plugins.

Before WiseLabs came FetOo, an electro/bass alias signed to 23 labels, played across Europe, and also at a festival for a 3,000+ crowd, with an exclusive track composed that same day, in a few hours, specifically for the show, alongside a flood of originals and remixes.

Stages shared with Kill The Noise, Benga, Proxy, Joe Ford, Arkasia, Son of Kick, Troublegum and Silent Frequencies. Releases landed on Heavy Artillery (US), Black Hole (NL), Kannibalen (CA), Pyramid (CA), Dirty Duck Audio (CA), Straight Up! (US), Jet Set Trash (UK), Freakz Me Out (DE), No Sense of Place (IT) and Phonon (FR), among others.
